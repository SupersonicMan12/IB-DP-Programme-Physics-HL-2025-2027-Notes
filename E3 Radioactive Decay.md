### The three types of radioactive decay
 **Alpha decay:** $${}^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y + {}^{4}_{2}\alpha$$
**Beta minus decay:**
$${}^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}Y + {}^{0}_{-1}\beta + \bar{\nu}$$
releasing an antineutrino. A neutron is swapped with a proton.

**Beta plus decay:**
$${}^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}Y + {}^{0}_{+1}\beta + \nu$$
releasing a neutrino. A proton is swapped with a neutron

**Gamma decay:** $${}^{A}_{Z}X^* \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z}X + \gamma$$

Penetration ability: $\gamma  > \beta > \alpha$
Ionization ability: $\alpha > \beta > \gamma$, ionization is dangerous and has many ethical issues, which IB cares most about.

Alpha particles and gamma photons always have discrete energies, beta particles have a continuous range of possible energies due to the extra freedom dimension added by the neutrinos.
- The spectrum for alpha and gamma radiation emissions provides evidence for the former; the continuous spectrum for beta decay is evidence for the neutrino: something in addition must have allowed this continuity to happen.

### Macro: Radioactive Decay

Radioactive decay is random - cannot be predicted - and spontaneous - we cannot affect the rate of decay in any way.

Activity = the number of decays per unit time for a sample of radioactive isotope. Activity decreases over time, unit: Bq.

Half-life $T_{\frac{1}{2}}$ = the amount of time it takes for half the nuclei in a sample to decay.

In a macro perspective, we describe radioactive decay as: $$N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t},$$where $\lambda$ is known as the decay constant. For activity, $A = \frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} = \lambda N = \lambda N_0 e^{-\lambda t}.$ From this we have $\lambda = \frac{\Delta N}{N \Delta t}$ =  the fraction of nuclei to decay per unit time. Thus, $\lambda$ approximates the probability of decay per sufficiently small unit time

We can connect $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$ to $N = N_0 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/T_{1/2}}$ by dividing and doing some math: $T_{1/2} = \frac{\ln{2}}{\lambda}.$

When doing calculations, we must take into consideration the effect of **Background Radiation**, which add a constant to $A$.

### Mass = Energy?

**Mass Defect** = the total mass of a stable nucleus is always less than the sum of the masses of its separate protons and neutrons: $\Delta m = Zm_p+Nm_n-m_{nucleus}$.

Another way to calculate it is through $E = mc^2$, where mass is expended to bind the nucleus.

**Binding Energy** $B_E$ = the work required to break apart a nucleus into separate nucleons. Average binding energy per nucleon is $\frac{B_E}{A}$.![[Screenshot 2026-06-12 at 20.16.03.png]]
