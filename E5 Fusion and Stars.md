Stars = plasma. Fusion occurs when two or more atomic nuclei join together, creating a new larger nucleus and releasing energy.

Star stability relies on: outwards radiation pressure balances inward gravitational forces.  

#### Fusion is a source of energy in stars:
Overall, $E=mc^2$ summarizes how moving upward in average binding energy (on the left side) leads to mass being lost.
![[Pasted image 20260612212946.png]]
The dominant process in most stars = p-p chain, where three stages occur; not tested, therefore, we do not learn it. The equation, however, is $6H \rightarrow He + 2H$.

Nuclear fusion requires nuclei to be really close to each other. There must be exceptionally extreme **conditions**: high **temperature**, high **pressure**.

To describe a star, we use Luminosity (L), Radius (r), Temperature (T), Mass (m), and we use a Hertzsprung-Russell (yes, German) diagram:

![[Screenshot 2026-06-12 at 21.33.24.png]]
Temperature vs. luminosity, logarithmic scale. Notice the position of our sun.

Two main things:

**The Effect of Stellar Mass on Evolution: **

Star = main sequence. Before this is always nebula then protostar.

Low mass stars (under 8 $m_s$) -> red giants -> planetary nebula -> whitedwarf

High mass stars (above 8 $m_s$) -> red supergiants -> supernova -> neutron star or black hole

Just know where each is placed on the HR diagram.
